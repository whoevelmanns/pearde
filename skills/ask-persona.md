name: ask-persona
description: Put one problem to one persona and talk to it until the question is settled — a named colleague with a field, a bias and a way of reading, pointed at this session's context, answering in their own voice and writing nothing. The roster covers engineering, product/design, teaching and adversarial review; a field it does not cover is researched and composed into a new persona first. Use for "ask <id> <question>", "/ask-persona", "what would the skeptic say", "get a second opinion on this", "review this as a designer", "poke holes in this", "who should I ask about this", "persona create <topic>", "make me a persona for <field>". Needs no board — it is a conversation, not a state change.

Read @references/parts/personas.md, then @references/personas/INDEX.md for the
roster. The scopes are `@@personas`.

- **One problem, one persona.** A call is aimed at a decision you are about to
  defend, not at a whole round. It answers, and it writes no file.
- **Never the one you are already wearing.** Asking yourself in a second
  context is not a second opinion.
- **It is a conversation.** Keep the persona you called and follow up in the
  same thread — the context it built is the point. A second dispatch is a
  second colleague with no memory of the first.
- **Relay it attributed.** Say who was asked and what came back, in their
  voice, including where they disagreed with you.
- **No persona for the field?** `persona create <topic>` — research the field
  and its real practitioners, compose one from the best of them, per
  @references/personas/INDEX.md. Then ask it.

Inside a board round this is the `ask <id> <question>` handle, and the
orchestrator reaches a persona on its own judgment as well — @@handles and
@references/parts/personas.md say when. Outside one, this skill is the whole
of it: nothing is scanned, nothing is dispatched, no state moves.
